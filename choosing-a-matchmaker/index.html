Choosing a matchmaker when you live abroad

A good matchmaker can save months of searching. A poor one can cost money and goodwill. These questions help you tell them apart before you sign anything.

What a professional matchmaker does

A matchmaker is a person or agency paid to find suitable partners for a client. For NRI families the work usually includes an interview with the client and often the parents, a written profile or biodata, a search of the matchmaker's own contacts and databases, and a first approach to the other family. Some matchmakers also coach clients before first meetings, arrange introductions in more than one country, or help with background checks.

What a matchmaker cannot do is guarantee a marriage. Be cautious of anyone who implies otherwise.

Who a matchmaker suits

  • Busy professionals who do not have time to search and screen people themselves.
  • Families who want a discreet, traditional process run by someone experienced.
  • People with specific requirements, such as a particular community, language or profession, who find general searches frustrating.
  • Divorced or widowed people who would like a careful, private approach to meeting someone new.

If you enjoy searching yourself and are comfortable with online services, you may not need one at all.

Questions to ask before you pay

About their experience

  • How long have you worked with NRI clients, and in which countries?
  • Do you work alone or with a team? Who will actually handle my search?
  • Do you specialise in particular communities, religions or regions?

About their method

  • Where do your introductions come from: your own clients, other matchmakers, online databases or community contacts?
  • How many introductions can I expect in a given period, and how are they chosen?
  • Do the people you introduce know they are being introduced, and have they agreed to it?
  • How do you verify the people you introduce?

About money and contracts

  • What exactly does the fee cover, and is anything charged on success?
  • How long does the agreement last, and can I pause or cancel it?
  • What happens to my information if I leave?
  • Will I receive a written contract before paying?

A professional matchmaker should be happy to answer all of these in writing. If you are in the UK and run into problems with a paid service, Citizens Advice consumer guidance explains your rights.

Warning signs

  • Pressure to pay a large fee immediately, or for a "limited-time" offer.
  • Promises of a guaranteed marriage or a set number of proposals.
  • No written contract or refund terms.
  • Reluctance to explain where introductions come from.
  • Requests to share passport scans, bank details or other sensitive documents early in the process.
  • Profiles of matches that look copied from elsewhere, or contact details that never seem to work.

Comparing a matchmaker with doing it yourself

A matchmaker gives you expertise and time; an online service gives you reach and control. Many people use both. You can, for example, post a matrimonial listing yourself and also ask matchmakers to approach you. On NRI Matrimony you can post a requirement describing the help you need, and matchmakers and other service providers reply with an enquiry that you can choose to answer, so your phone number and email stay private.

Making the first meeting productive

Go to your first consultation with a short written summary: your background, what you are looking for, your non-negotiables, where you are willing to live, and how involved your family will be. Ask the matchmaker to repeat back what they have understood. A good one will challenge unrealistic expectations politely; a poor one will simply agree with everything.

Frequently asked questions

Are NRI matchmakers expensive?

Prices vary widely, from modest one-off fees to large packages. Compare what is included rather than the headline price, and never pay without written terms.

Can a matchmaker work with my parents rather than me?

Yes, many work with families. Agree beforehand who the matchmaker reports to and who decides whether to accept an introduction.

Should a matchmaker run background checks?

Some offer checks or work with verification companies. Ask what is checked and how. You can also arrange checks yourself, as our safety guide explains.

What if I am not happy with the introductions?

Give specific feedback early. If nothing improves, use the cancellation terms in your contract.
