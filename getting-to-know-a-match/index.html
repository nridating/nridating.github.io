Getting to know a match, from first message to first meeting

An introduction is only the start. These steps help you learn whether a match is right for you, even when you live in different countries.

Start slowly and honestly

After an introduction, there is often pressure to decide quickly, especially when families are involved. Resist it. The first few weeks are for finding out whether you enjoy talking to each other and whether your plans fit. Both of you should feel free to say "this is not right for me" at any point, politely and without blame.

Begin with messages on the platform or through the matchmaker, rather than personal numbers or social media. It keeps your details private until you are comfortable and makes it easier to step back if you need to.

First messages

A good first message refers to something specific in the other person's profile and asks an open question. "You mentioned you moved to Auckland for work. What surprised you most about living there?" invites a real answer. "Hi, how are you?" usually does not.

Keep early conversations light but genuine: work, family, hobbies, food, the places you have lived. You will learn a great deal about someone from how they talk about their family and their past.

Move to video early

Once you have exchanged a few messages and want to continue, suggest a video call within the first couple of weeks. It confirms the person is who they say they are, and it tells you more in twenty minutes than a month of texting. Someone who repeatedly avoids video calls with excuses deserves caution; see our safety guide.

Managing time zones

  • Agree a regular time that suits both of you, such as early evening for one person and morning for the other.
  • Keep calls short at first, around half an hour, so they never feel like a chore.
  • Use a shared calendar or simply confirm each call the day before.

Questions worth asking, gradually

You do not need to cover everything in the first call, but over several conversations these topics matter:

  • Where do you see yourself living in five years, and how fixed is that?
  • How do you picture our relationship with each other's families?
  • How do you share money and household responsibilities?
  • What role does religion or tradition play in your daily life?
  • Do you want children, and if so, roughly when?
  • How do you handle disagreements?

Answers do not have to match perfectly. What matters is whether you can discuss differences calmly and find a way forward together.

When to involve families

Some families want to speak early; others wait until the couple is sure. Agree the timing together. A relaxed video call where parents simply say hello can be a good intermediate step before formal meetings. Our family guide covers how to balance everyone's expectations.

Planning a first meeting

  • Meet in a public place, such as a café or restaurant, and tell a friend or relative where you will be.
  • Arrange your own travel and accommodation if you are travelling to another city or country.
  • Keep the first meeting short and low-pressure; a long weekend together is too much for a first meeting.
  • Never agree to carry anything or pay for anything on someone else's behalf.

Dating first, deciding later

Not every introduction has to begin with marriage on the table. Many NRIs, particularly those who grew up abroad, prefer to meet people more casually and let a relationship develop. If that suits you better, a dating-first service such as NRI Dating lets you meet other Indians abroad through private conversations and decide later whether you are heading towards marriage.

Frequently asked questions

How long should we talk before meeting?

There is no rule. Many couples meet within one to three months of regular conversation, once they have spoken on video several times and feel comfortable.

Is it a bad sign if a match wants to move off the platform quickly?

Not always, but be careful. Scammers often push for private chat apps early. Stay on the platform until you have video-called and trust the person.

What if our families disagree?

Listen to their concerns and discuss them openly with your match. Meeting each other's families on video often helps.

How do I end things politely?

Be brief, kind and clear: thank them for their time and say you do not feel it is the right match. You do not need to give detailed reasons.
