Safety and verification in NRI matchmaking

Most people you meet are genuine. A few simple checks protect you and your family from the small number who are not.

Why NRIs are targeted

Romance and marriage scams are reported in every country with a large Indian diaspora. NRIs are attractive targets because they are often assumed to have savings, they may be searching across borders where checks are harder, and families can feel pressure to move quickly once an introduction looks promising. The good news is that scams follow predictable patterns, and a few habits stop most of them.

Warning signs

  • The person avoids video calls, or the camera is always "broken".
  • They profess strong feelings or talk about marriage very quickly.
  • Their story changes: job, city or family details do not stay consistent.
  • They push to move the conversation to a private app or personal email very early.
  • A sudden emergency appears: a medical bill, customs fee, frozen bank account or travel cost.
  • They ask for money, gift cards, cryptocurrency, or help receiving or forwarding money or parcels.
  • They ask for copies of your passport, visa or bank statements.

Any request for money from someone you have not met in person is a reason to stop and talk to someone you trust.

Simple checks you can do yourself

  1. Video call early. It is the single most effective check.
  2. Check consistency. Do the name, photos, profession and location match across what they have told you?
  3. Search their photos. A reverse image search can show whether photos have been taken from someone else.
  4. Look for a real footprint. A professional networking profile, a workplace listing or mutual contacts can help confirm identity, though their absence alone is not proof of anything.
  5. Involve family or a friend. A second opinion is valuable, especially when you feel emotionally invested.
  6. Use platform tools. Prefer services that review listings, offer ID verification and let you block and report members.

Professional background verification

Some families arrange a professional check before an engagement, particularly when the couple has met online or lives in different countries. Verification companies can confirm details such as identity, education, employment and sometimes marital history, within the limits of local law and with appropriate consent. Ask exactly what will be checked, how, and what the report will contain, and make sure the check is proportionate and respectful of the other person's privacy.

Some matrimonial services also let you post a requirement for background verification. On NRI Matrimony, every listing is reviewed by a person before it goes live, listing photos are blurred for other members, and you can post a requirement for services such as background verification, with providers replying through private messages.

Where to report scams

  • United Kingdom: Action Fraud, the national reporting centre for fraud and cybercrime.
  • Australia: Scamwatch's romance scam guidance.
  • Canada: the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.
  • Indian nationals abroad: the Ministry of External Affairs has information on marital issues involving overseas Indians.

Also report the person to the platform or matchmaker where you met them, so they can protect others.

Forced marriage

A marriage must be entered freely by both people. If you or someone you know is being pressured into marriage, help is available. The UK government's forced marriage guidance explains how to contact the Forced Marriage Unit confidentially, including when the marriage would take place abroad.

Frequently asked questions

Is it rude to ask a match for a video call?

No. It is a normal, sensible step, and genuine matches usually welcome it.

Should I run a background check on every match?

Usually not. Basic checks and time are enough for most introductions. A professional check may be worth considering before an engagement, especially across countries.

What should I do if I have already sent money?

Stop all payments, contact your bank immediately, keep all messages and report it to the fraud reporting service in your country and to the platform.

Can a scammer use a real person's photos?

Yes, which is why a live video call matters more than photos.
